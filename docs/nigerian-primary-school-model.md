# Nigerian Primary School Academic & Assessment Model (NERDC / UBEC)

## 1. Statutory Framework & Institutional Hierarchy
In Nigeria, primary education is governed federally by the **Universal Basic Education Commission (UBEC)** and the **Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council (NERDC)** under the 9-Year Basic Education Curriculum (BEC):
- **Pre-Primary / Early Childhood (ECCDE)**: Creche, Nursery 1–2 / Kindergarten (Ages 3–5). Distinct developmental stage focusing on cognitive development, psychomotor skills, phonics, and socialization.
- **Lower Basic**: Primary 1 to Primary 3 (Ages 6–8).
- **Middle Basic**: Primary 4 to Primary 6 (Ages 9–11).
- **Upper Basic**: Junior Secondary (JSS 1 to JSS 3).

## 2. The Class Teacher (Homeroom) Model vs. Secondary Subject Teacher Model
- **Primary School System**:
  - Operates on a **Class Teacher System**.
  - A single Class Teacher (Form Teacher) is assigned to a specific classroom (e.g., Primary 4).
  - The Class Teacher teaches and manages the continuous assessments and examinations across **all subjects** for that classroom's enrolled pupils.
  - **Class Assignment IS Subject Assignment**: Primary teachers are not assigned individual course IDs; assigning a teacher to "Primary 4" grants full grading authority for all subjects offered by Primary 4.
- **Secondary School System**:
  - Operates on a **Departmental / Subject Teacher System**.
  - Subject teachers are specialists assigned to specific subjects across multiple arms/classes (e.g., Chemistry teacher for SS 1A, SS 1B, SS 2).

## 3. Standard Subjects under NERDC Middle Basic (Primary 4)
In Nigerian primary education, subjects **do not have course codes** (such as `MATH101` or `ENG201`). Courses are identified purely by their official descriptive curriculum titles:
1. **English Studies** (incorporating Grammar, Comprehension, Composition, and Reading)
2. **Mathematics** / **General Mathematics**
3. **Basic Science & Technology** (BST)
4. **Information Technology (Computer Studies / ICT)**
5. **Physical & Health Education** (PHE)
6. **Social Studies**
7. **Civic Education / National Values**
8. **Christian Religious Studies (CRS)** / **Islamic Religious Studies (IRS)**
9. **Cultural & Creative Arts (CCA)**
10. **Agricultural Science**
11. **Home Economics**
12. **Nigerian Languages** (Yoruba, Igbo, or Hausa)
13. **French Language** (optional introductory)

## 4. Assessment & Gradebook Structure (NERDC Standard)
- **Continuous Assessment 1 (1st CA)**: 20 Marks (Tests, assignments, practicals)
- **Continuous Assessment 2 (2nd CA)**: 20 Marks (Mid-term test, class projects)
- **Terminal Examination**: 60 Marks
- **Total Terminal Score**: 100 Marks ($20 + 20 + 60$)
- **Grading Scale (Standard BEC)**:
  - **75% – 100%**: A (Distinction / Excellent)
  - **65% – 74%**: B (Very Good)
  - **50% – 64%**: C (Credit / Good)
  - **40% – 49%**: P (Pass / Fair)
  - **0% – 39%**: F (Fail)
- **Positioning**:
  - **Subject Position**: Ordinal rank (1st, 2nd, 3rd...) of pupils within that specific subject based on Total Score descending.
  - **Class Position**: Cumulative broadsheet ranking calculated from the aggregate of all subjects at the end of the term.
- **Remarks**: Descriptive evaluation matched to performance tier (e.g., "Excellent achievement", "Very good effort", "Satisfactory progress", "Needs improvement").

## 5. Assessment Entry Workflow in Primary
The class teacher enters assessments **subject-by-subject** for the identical cohort of pupils:
1. Teacher opens Gradebook for their assigned class (`Primary 4`).
2. Teacher selects a Subject from the dropdown (e.g., `General Mathematics`).
3. Teacher enters CA1 (max 20), CA2 (max 20), Exam (max 60) for all pupils in the class.
4. The system calculates Total, Grade, Position, and Remark in real-time.
5. Teacher saves scores.
6. Teacher switches the Subject dropdown to the next subject, and the gradebook reloads the scores for that subject while preserving Class, Term, and Session.
